Wedding-deposit payment router

Wedding Deposit Router

Splits a wedding deposit-plus-instalment payment — however many rows, however many cardholders — across whichever miles cards earn the most for your venue's merchant category code (MCC), filling each bonus-cap tier before falling back to a flat uncapped card. Built for Singapore-issued miles cards; edit the card roster to match your own wallet. Everything below is editable and persisted in your browser only — nothing is sent anywhere.

1 · Merchant category (MCC) bucket

Hotel-ballroom venues typically bill under MCC 7011; standalone restaurants under 5812. MCCs aren't published by merchants and vary by venue — a hotel-linked restaurant can bill as either. The reliable way to confirm one: lock/freeze a card in its banking app, have the venue attempt a small charge, and read the MCC off the failed-transaction notification. Don't commit a card strategy to an assumption on a large payment.

3 · Card roster

Bonus rate applies to spend under the monthly cap (blank = uncapped) while a checked MCC box matches the bucket above. "Any" means the card is MCC-agnostic (routes everything at its listed rate, e.g. via an online-spend converter). Every card also earns its base rate as a flat, unlimited fallback — what a maxed-out card, or a non-matching card, reverts to. Starter roster reflects publicly available Singapore card terms as a starting point — issuers change rates and caps every few months, so re-check before relying on this for a real payment.

Model: each scheduled payment is a fractional-knapsack problem — every dollar can go to any card's bonus tier (capacity = its monthly cap) or its uncapped base rate (capacity = unlimited), each with a fixed miles-per-dollar value, and the row's due amount has to land somewhere. Sorting every chunk by rate descending and filling the highest first is the textbook-optimal solution to that problem shape: an exchange argument shows any allocation that left a higher-rate chunk with room while using a lower-rate one could always be improved by shifting a dollar across, so greedy-by-rate can't be beaten here. Caps reset every row — treat each row as its own statement cycle, with no interaction between rows.

Confirm the venue actually accepts the deposit/balance being split across multiple card charges before relying on this plan, and ask about a credit-card surcharge (commonly 1–3% on large SG hospitality invoices) — it can easily exceed the value of any extra miles from bonus-category chasing. Re-verify each card's bonus rate/cap, and your venue's real MCC, close to the payment date: SG issuers revise these every few months, and MCCs are set by the merchant's acquirer, not published, so a confirmed value today can still change if the venue switches processor.
